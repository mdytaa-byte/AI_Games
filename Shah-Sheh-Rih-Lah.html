How to play

From the novel: Shah-Sheh-Rih-Lah is a two-player wagering game. Players chant five beats while forming a fixed sign on each syllable, then make a silent throw into one of four choice signs. A secret number of golden merits is clenched in the other hand. The winner takes both hidden wagers.

The chant

Begin in Hah — palm upright, four fingers together, thumb extended upward. Speak and sign Shah, Sheh, Rih, Lah, Hah. Immediately after the fifth beat comes the silent throw.

The four choice signs

ShahThree fingers pointed at the opponent; thumb touches the little finger. Beats Sheh.
ShehIndex and middle together toward the opponent; the rest folded. Beats Lah.
LahIndex and middle in a narrow V; the rest folded. Beats Rih.
RihThumb and little finger extended, wrist rotated thumb-up. Beats Shah.

Winning, losing, drawing

  • Shah defeats Sheh, Sheh defeats Lah, Lah defeats Rih, Rih defeats Shah.
  • Matching signs draw. The opposite pairs also draw: Shah against Lah, and Sheh against Rih.
  • A draw forces another throw. The hidden wagers stay clenched until someone wins.
  • A decisive winner takes both wagers. Matches are often played to the first win, or until a belt is emptied.

The Hah mask

The fifth beat is ceremony — and a tell. Players who have already chosen often crack, just slightly, on Hah. A Sightless ear can hear which throw is coming. Sailors who train against that ear keep the voice even, or hitch toward a false sign to bait the counter.

Set Even voice to hide your throw, or hitch toward a lie. On Hah the hands twitch and the chant hangs: believe the hitch, ignore it, or change your own throw before the silence. In the full sequence the tell sits on Akh.

The full sequence

The old rite does not stop at Hah. Toggle Full sequence to chant all seven Scourge names — Shah, Sheh, Rih, Lah, Hah, Ehl, Akh — and throw afterward from six signs. Ehl raises the right arm overhead, palm open. Akh raises the left to join it.

EhlRight arm to the sky. Beats Shah and Sheh. Draws with Akh.
AkhBoth hands overhead. Beats Lah and Rih. Draws with Ehl.

The sailors’ four-sign cycle is unchanged. Shah and Sheh still cut down Akh; Lah and Rih still drag Ehl back to the table.

Custom

Speaking the name of the game to someone wearing a merit belt is a formal challenge. Witnesses may officiate. After a win, extend a hand; the other player presses the back of their hand to your palm.

Codex of the signs
