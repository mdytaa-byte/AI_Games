What improved in v3 — Step 19?

Step 19 rebuilds the character silhouettes so they read as illustrated school-anime figures instead of assembled polygons. Masses share one outer ink contour. The default teen proportion is a fuller torso and sleeve against a smaller hair globe, and each outfit cuts a different outer shape instead of sitting as a sticker on one body.

  • Hair, face, sleeve, torso, and hand no longer each stroke themselves, so overlaps are not double-outlined
  • Head and hair scale sit smaller against broader shoulders, so the jacket — not the hair mass — reads as the widest form
  • Sleeves keep volume from deltoid to cuff, with a rounded cap where the arm leaves the jacket
  • Torso, neck, and limb paths use anatomical tapers, hidden joint roots, and soft end-caps instead of capsule tubes
  • Clothes reshape the outer contour: blazer V and peaked shoulders, sailor collar flaps, gakuran stand collar, cardigan hem, t-shirt length, apron, and yukata drape — not one body with decals on top
  • Hands are one palm mass: fingers grow out of a knuckle pad with webbing, not five separate sticks
  • Hair is denser: overlapping bangs, undercut framing locks, inner strand edges, and a forehead hairline seam
  • The face silhouette uses a multi-point jaw curve so cheeks no longer read as a rounded hexagon
  • Shoes, backpacks, scarves, and held props follow the same illustration language instead of rectangles and blobs
  • All existing sliders — exaggeration, art direction, pose, folds, depth, hair motion, expression, and anatomy — still drive the new drawing
